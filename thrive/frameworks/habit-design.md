# Habit Design

You don't rise to the level of your goals. You fall to the level of your systems. This is the file for *engineering* habits that survive contact with real life.

---

## The behavior equation (BJ Fogg)

> **B = MAP**
>
> *Behavior happens when **Motivation**, **Ability**, and a **Prompt** converge at the same moment.*

If a habit isn't sticking, exactly one of these three is missing:

- **Motivation low?** Don't try to "be more motivated" — make the habit smaller. Motivation fluctuates; ability is engineering.
- **Ability low?** Shrink the habit until it's stupidly easy. "Floss one tooth." "Do one push-up." Then grow it.
- **Prompt missing?** Anchor it to an existing habit. *After [existing habit], I will [new habit].*

## The 4 laws (James Clear)

To **build** a habit, make it:
1. **Obvious** — design your environment so the cue is unmissable. Running shoes by the bed.
2. **Attractive** — pair the habit with something you enjoy (temptation bundling). Podcast only during gym.
3. **Easy** — 2-minute rule. New habits start in their smallest form. "Read 1 page." "Open the doc."
4. **Satisfying** — reward yourself immediately after. Track with a chain you don't want to break (Seinfeld method).

To **break** a habit, invert each:
1. Make it **invisible** (remove the cue — phone out of bedroom).
2. Make it **unattractive** (highlight costs — write down what the habit costs you in 5 years).
3. Make it **difficult** (add friction — log out of social, delete the app, leave junk food out of the house).
4. Make it **unsatisfying** (accountability partner, public commitment).

## Identity-based habits (the lever)

The most powerful shift: don't focus on the *outcome*. Focus on the *identity*.

- ❌ "I want to lose 10 kg." (outcome)
- ❌ "I'll run 3× a week." (process)
- ✅ "I am a runner." (identity)

Then every action is a vote for that identity. Skipping today is a vote against. Showing up — even 5 minutes — is a vote for. Identity wins the long game because it's self-reinforcing: as you act like a runner, you become one, which makes acting like one easier.

**Question to ask yourself before any decision:** *"What would [the person I'm becoming] do right now?"*

## The 2-minute rule

A new habit should take less than 2 minutes to start.
- "Read before bed" → "Read one page."
- "Train every day" → "Put on workout clothes."
- "Meditate" → "Sit and take 3 breaths."

You're not trying to do the habit. You're trying to *become someone who shows up*. Once that's automatic, scaling is easy.

## Habit stacking

Anchor new habits to an existing, automatic one:

> *After I pour my morning coffee, I will write 3 things I'm grateful for.*

The existing habit is the prompt. The location/time is decided for you. No decision fatigue.

## Environment > willpower

You will not out-willpower your environment. Long-term.

- Want to eat better? Don't buy junk food. You can't eat what isn't there at 11pm.
- Want to read more? Phone in another room. Book on the pillow.
- Want to scroll less? Greyscale phone, app timers, log out of accounts on the browser.
- Want to train consistently? Gym 5 min from home, not 30.

**Design the environment so the right thing is the easy thing.**

## Streaks, slip-ups, and never miss twice

- A streak is powerful — *until* it's broken. Then "since I broke it, why bother."
- Rule: **never miss twice in a row.** One miss is an event. Two misses is the start of a new habit (the wrong one).
- Make the *minimum viable habit* even on bad days. Sick? "Just put on workout clothes and sit on the floor for 1 minute." The chain stays alive.

## Trackers that actually work

- Paper chain calendar (Seinfeld). Cross off the day. Don't break the chain.
- Simple notes app: date + "✅" per habit. No app needed.
- For deep work / training: a tally counter, a notebook, an Apple Watch ring.

Avoid: 14 habits in a fancy app. You'll quit by week 3. Pick 1–3 keystone habits and own them.

## Keystone habits

Some habits trigger cascading positive change. Pick yours:

- **Sleep at consistent time** → energy → training → diet → mood → work.
- **Daily walk** → reduces stress → eat less → sleep better.
- **Morning training** → momentum carries → fewer impulsive decisions later.
- **Weekly review (15 min Sunday)** → priorities clear → less scattered week.

Install one keystone. The downstream habits get easier.

## The 1% framing

1% improvement per day = 37× better in a year. 1% decline per day = near zero in a year. Compounding is non-intuitive — the back end of the curve is where the magic is.

You won't see results at week 1. You won't see them at week 4. By month 6, things change. By year 2, people who knew you don't recognize you. The job is *not* to see results. The job is to be the person who shows up while the results bake.

## Diagnostic — "why isn't this habit sticking?"

Walk down this list in order:

1. Is the habit too big? Shrink it.
2. Is the cue clear and at the right time? Anchor it.
3. Is your environment fighting you? Redesign it.
4. Is the identity in place? "I am a person who..."
5. Did you miss twice? Forgive yourself, re-start at the smallest version, today.
6. Is the habit even worth keeping? If not, drop it without guilt.
