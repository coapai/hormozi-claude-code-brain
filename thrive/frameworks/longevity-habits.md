# Longevity Habits — Top 10

What actually predicts a long, *healthy* life. Ranked by evidence strength × effect size × accessibility. The first five do ~80% of the work.

---

## 1. Don't smoke. Don't get fat around the middle.

The two largest reversible mortality risks. Visceral fat (waist > half your height in inches) is metabolically active and drives insulin resistance, inflammation, cardiovascular disease.

**Do this:** waist-to-height ratio < 0.5. Track once a month. Everything else listed below will help you get there.

## 2. Strength + Zone 2 cardio, weekly, for life.

- Resistance training 2–4×/week. Muscle mass and grip strength are top-tier predictors of all-cause mortality past 60.
- Zone 2 cardio 3+ hours/week. Builds mitochondria — the cellular engine of metabolic health.
- VO2max in the top quartile for your age ≈ ~5× lower all-cause mortality vs. bottom quartile (Mandsager et al., 2018, Cleveland Clinic, JAMA).

**Do this:** 3× strength + 3× zone 2 cardio + 1× VO2max interval session per week. Non-negotiable as brushing teeth.

## 3. Sleep 7–9 hours, regularly.

Both *short* (<6h) and *irregular* sleep correlate with higher all-cause mortality. Sleep is when the brain clears amyloid-beta (Alzheimer's), when muscle repairs, when hormones reset.

**Do this:** fixed wake time. Bedroom dark + cool. No screens 60 min before bed. (See `protocols/sleep-optimization.md`.)

## 4. Eat mostly plants + adequate protein. Stop before stuffed.

The diets of all Blue Zones (Okinawa, Sardinia, Nicoya, Ikaria, Loma Linda) share: heavy on vegetables/legumes, whole grains, moderate animal protein, minimally processed.

- 30+ g fiber/day.
- 0.7–1.0 g protein per lb bodyweight (especially after 50, to fight sarcopenia).
- "Hara hachi bu" — stop at 80% full.

**Do this:** double vegetables, hit protein target, cut ultra-processed food <10% of calories.

## 5. Strong social bonds. Active purpose.

- Harvard Study of Adult Development (85 years running): the strongest predictor of health at 80 was relationship satisfaction at 50.
- Loneliness ≈ 15 cigarettes/day for mortality risk (Holt-Lunstad meta-analysis).
- Okinawan *ikigai*, Costa Rican *plan de vida*: a reason to get up correlates with 7+ extra healthy years.

**Do this:** one weekly "deep" social contact (>1 hour, in person), one weekly call to a parent/old friend. Have an answer to "what am I building toward?" — re-write it every January.

---

## 6. Sun in the morning. Move after meals.

- 5–15 min of outdoor light in the first hour of waking → entrained circadian rhythm → better sleep that night, better mood, better metabolism.
- A 10-min walk after meals lowers post-meal glucose ~30%. Cumulatively huge over 50 years.

## 7. Sauna + cold, used sparingly.

- 4× sauna/week, 20+ min at 80°C+ → ~40% lower all-cause mortality, 60% lower cardiovascular death (Laukkanen et al., Finnish cohort).
- Cold: less hard evidence on longevity, real evidence on dopamine, mood, and stress tolerance. 2–3 min, 2–4×/week.

## 8. Limit alcohol. Seriously.

No amount is "good for you" (the old red wine claim has been retracted by larger meta-analyses). Each drink/day above zero raises risk of cancer, atrial fibrillation, dementia.

**Do this:** ≤ 3 drinks/week or zero. Never daily.

## 9. Lifelong learning. Cognitive load.

Brains that work, last. Reading, language learning, complex skills, social engagement → ~30% lower dementia risk vs. cognitively inactive seniors.

**Do this:** one book/month, one new skill/year, one cognitively demanding hobby (chess, instrument, language, coding).

## 10. Test, don't guess.

Annual blood panel. DEXA scan once. Know your numbers: ApoB, fasting insulin, HbA1c, hs-CRP, testosterone (men), thyroid, vitamin D.

You can't fix what you don't measure. By the time symptoms show up, the disease has been brewing for a decade.

---

## What's NOT on this list (and why)

- **Supplements.** Almost everything is unnecessary if 1–9 are dialed. Worth considering: creatine 5g/day, vitamin D if deficient, omega-3 if no fatty fish. The rest is marketing.
- **Intermittent fasting.** Works for weight loss because it cuts calories. No solid evidence of longevity benefit beyond that.
- **Cleanses, detoxes.** Your liver does this. Stop.
- **Expensive longevity gadgets.** Sleep tracker can be useful. Continuous glucose monitor for 1 month is educational. Everything else is optional.

## Diagnostic question

> "Of these 10, which 3 are you weakest on?"

That's the answer. Don't optimize #7 if #1, #3, and #5 are broken.
