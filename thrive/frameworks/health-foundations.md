# Health Foundations

Five inputs explain ~80% of how someone feels and how long they stay healthy. Fix these in order. Don't optimize #5 if #1 is broken.

## 1. Sleep (the multiplier)

- **Target:** 7–9 hours, same wake time ±30 min every day (including weekends).
- **Why it dominates:** one bad night ↓ insulin sensitivity ~25%, ↓ testosterone ~10–15%, ↑ cortisol next day, impairs memory consolidation, raises hunger hormones (ghrelin).
- **Non-negotiables:**
  - Cool room (18–19°C / 65–67°F).
  - No bright light 60–90 min before bed.
  - No caffeine after ~10h before bedtime (caffeine half-life ≈ 5–7h).
  - No food/alcohol 2–3h before bed.
  - Wake time fixed → bedtime adjusts.
- **Measure:** total sleep time + how you feel at 10am without caffeine.
- Reference: Matthew Walker, *Why We Sleep*.

## 2. Movement (training)

Two non-negotiables. Most people skip one and wonder why progress is flat.

- **Zone 2 cardio** — 3–4 hours/week. "Can hold a conversation, slightly uncomfortable." Builds mitochondrial density, the engine of metabolic health.
- **Resistance training** — 2–4 sessions/week, compound lifts (squat, hinge, push, pull, carry). Builds muscle = the largest predictor of healthspan past 50.
- **Plus:** 8–10k steps/day baseline. Walking after meals (10 min) blunts glucose spikes.
- **Optional, high ROI:** 1 VO2 max session/week (4×4 intervals).
- Reference: Peter Attia, *Outlive*.

## 3. Nutrition (4 levers, in order)

1. **Protein** — 0.7–1.0 g per lb bodyweight (1.6–2.2 g/kg). Most adults under-eat protein and over-eat refined carbs.
2. **Whole foods** — if it has an ingredient list >5 items or words you can't pronounce, default no.
3. **Fiber** — 30+ g/day. Vegetables, legumes, fruit, oats. The single most under-prioritized lever for gut health and satiety.
4. **Hydration** — body-weight-in-lbs ÷ 2 = ounces water/day. Add electrolytes if training hard or sweating.

Skip the "perfect diet" debate. Mediterranean / whole-foods / high-protein-omnivore all work. **What's consistently sustainable beats what's theoretically optimal.**

## 4. Recovery & stress

- **Sun on skin in the first hour after waking.** 5–15 min sets the circadian clock (cortisol peak → melatonin onset 14–16h later).
- **Breathwork** — 5 min of slow nasal breathing (e.g. 4-in / 6-out) drops sympathetic tone. Free, works, no excuse.
- **Sauna** — 4× per week, 20 min at 80°C+ is associated with 40% lower all-cause mortality in Finnish cohort studies. Heat shock proteins, cardiovascular adaptation.
- **Cold** — 2–3 min cold exposure 2–4×/week. Mostly mental — dopamine baseline, stress resilience.
- **Social connection** — Harvard Study of Adult Development: relationship quality at 50 predicted health at 80 better than cholesterol did. Schedule it like training.

## 5. Biomarkers (test, don't guess)

Ask a doctor for these annually after 30:

- **Lipids:** ApoB (better than LDL-C), Lp(a) once in lifetime.
- **Metabolic:** fasting insulin, HbA1c, fasting glucose, triglycerides.
- **Inflammation:** hs-CRP, homocysteine.
- **Hormones:** total + free testosterone (men), full thyroid panel, DHEA, vitamin D (25-OH).
- **Body comp:** DEXA scan once → know visceral fat + lean mass, not just weight.

Numbers in normal "lab range" are not the same as optimal. Optimal ApoB is closer to <60 mg/dL than to the lab's "<100".

## Diagnostic questions (ask before prescribing)

1. How many hours did you sleep last night? Average over 7 days?
2. Strength training & cardio per week?
3. Grams of protein on a typical day?
4. Resting heart rate, last blood panel?
5. What's the one habit you'd keep if you could only keep one?

The user's answers tell you which lever to pull first.
