# Sleep Optimization Protocol

Sleep is not negotiable. It is the foundation under every other lever — training, diet, mindset, productivity, mood. Fix this first. Most "low energy" problems are sleep problems wearing a costume.

---

## Targets

- **Duration:** 7–9 hours actual sleep (in bed for 7.5–9.5h).
- **Consistency:** wake time fixed ±30 min, including weekends. Bedtime adjusts to hit duration.
- **Quality markers:** falls asleep <20 min, ≥1 cycle of deep sleep (90 min total), wakes feeling rested without a snooze.

---

## The 10 rules (in order of impact)

### 1. Fixed wake time, 7 days a week.

The single highest-leverage move. Your circadian clock anchors on *when you wake*, not when you sleep. Weekend "catch-up sleep" gives you social jetlag every Monday. Pick a wake time you can keep on Saturday — that's your number.

### 2. Sun on skin and eyes within the first hour of waking.

5–15 min of outdoor light → cortisol awakening response peaks correctly → melatonin onset 14–16h later → you're sleepy at the right time tonight. Free. Works. Not optional.

### 3. Last caffeine 8–10 hours before bed.

Caffeine half-life ≈ 5–7 hours. Quarter-life ≈ 10–14h. That 4pm coffee is still ~25% active at midnight, fragmenting deep sleep — even if you "fell asleep fine."

Hard cutoff: **noon for most people.** 2pm if you metabolize fast.

### 4. Cool the room: 18–19°C / 65–67°F.

Body temperature must drop ~1°C to initiate sleep. Cold room helps. Hot bath/shower 60–90 min before bed helps (the post-shower drop in core temperature mimics the natural drop).

### 5. Dim and dark.

- Bedroom: blackout curtains or sleep mask. Aim for "can't see your hand in front of your face" dark.
- Last 60–90 min: dim lights in the home. Lamps not overheads. Warm-tone bulbs not blue.
- Phones/screens: night mode, brightness low, ideally not in bedroom at all.

Bright light at night suppresses melatonin by up to 50%.

### 6. No food or alcohol within 2–3 hours of bed.

- Alcohol fragments REM and deep sleep even at small doses, even if it "knocks you out."
- Late meals raise core body temp and require digestive work — both fight sleep onset.
- Hungry? A small protein/fat snack (Greek yogurt, almonds) is fine. Avoid sugar.

### 7. Wind-down ritual, 30–60 min.

Same sequence every night → trains the brain that sleep is coming.
- Lights dim.
- Phone off / out of room (or in airplane mode, face down).
- Hot shower / bath.
- Read paper book (fiction works best — analytical books wake you up).
- Stretching, breathwork, journaling — pick one.

### 8. Bed is for two things only.

Sleep and sex. Not work, not scrolling, not eating, not arguing. The brain associates the bed with whatever you do there. If you toss for >20 min, get out, sit in dim light, read until sleepy, return.

### 9. Magnesium glycinate + chamomile/tart cherry (optional).

If lifestyle is dialed and sleep is still rough, *consider* (not prescription):
- Magnesium glycinate at night — calming, low side effects.
- Tart cherry juice — small bump in melatonin.
- L-theanine 100–200 mg if anxious.
- Avoid: melatonin in high doses (>1 mg). Body makes ~0.3 mg/night naturally. More isn't better, and tolerance builds fast.

### 10. Track once, then trust the protocol.

A sleep tracker (Oura, Whoop, Apple Watch) for 2–4 weeks is educational. After that, your subjective rating at 10am — *do I feel rested without caffeine?* — is the only metric that matters. Don't become a slave to the score.

## If you can't fall asleep

- Don't fight it. Get out of bed. Sit in another dim room.
- Read a paper book. Boring works. Math textbook is gold.
- Breathwork: 4-7-8 breathing (inhale 4, hold 7, exhale 8) × 4 rounds.
- Back to bed when eyes are heavy.

Cause is usually one of: caffeine too late, screen/light too late, stress not unwound, room too warm, ate too late.

## If you wake at 3am and can't get back

- Common between 2–4am ("cortisol nadir"). Often a sign of alcohol, late food, or stress.
- Don't check phone. Don't check the clock (creates anxiety about hours remaining).
- Breath: nasal, slow, 4-in / 6-out. Body relaxes; sleep follows.
- If wide awake after 20 min — get up, read, dim light, return.

## Troubleshooting decision tree

| Problem | First check |
|---|---|
| Can't fall asleep | Caffeine after noon? Phone in bed? Room too warm? |
| Wake at 3am | Alcohol last night? Late heavy meal? Unprocessed stress? |
| Wake unrefreshed despite 8h | Quality issue: sleep apnea? Room not dark? Alcohol? Consistent wake time? |
| Tired all afternoon | Probably caffeine after noon, or under-eating protein at breakfast, or short on light/movement. |
| Sleep is fine, mornings are slow | Delay caffeine 90 min after waking; sun on skin within 30 min of waking. |

---

## When to see a doctor

- Loud snoring, gasping awake, daytime sleepiness despite enough hours → possible sleep apnea, get a sleep study.
- Chronic insomnia >3 months despite the protocol → cognitive behavioral therapy for insomnia (CBT-I) has the best evidence base of any intervention, including drugs.
- Restless legs, parasomnias, vivid nightmares persistent → check in.

This protocol fixes lifestyle drivers, not pathology.
